Noom Monthly vs Annual Plans: Which One Costs Less?

The full price list, the maths, and the catch. Prices checked 22 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: The annual plan is cheapest per month at $17.40, paid as $209 up front. Month-to-month is $70 a month. The 4-month plan most people pick is $169 up front, $42.25 a month. All plans are prepaid and auto-renew. The catch: the cheapest plan is only cheapest if you use it — a $209 annual plan you abandon after a month costs you more than a $70 month. For adults 18 and over only.

That last sentence is the whole article in one line. Noom’s price list is a ladder: the longer you commit, the less you pay per month. The ladder is real, the savings are real, and the trap is real too — the savings only exist if you stay on the plan. This guide lays out all twelve plans, works out what each one actually costs, and shows the maths for the three plans that matter: monthly, 4-month, and annual. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide is about money, not about what the program will do for you.

The three plans that matter

Twelve plans is a lot of choice, and most of it is noise. Three plans carry the decision: the month-to-month plan at $70 a month, the 4-month plan at $169 ($42.25 a month), and the 12-month plan at $209 ($17.40 a month). The 4-month plan is the one most Noom Weight users have, according to Noom’s own page. The other nine plans fill the gaps between these three, and their monthly averages fall on a smooth slope between $70 and $17.40 — there is no hidden bargain hiding at 7 or 9 months (checked 22 November 2026).

The rest of this guide compares those three. If you want the full program — what the lessons teach, what the coach does, how the colors work — those are in The Complete Noom Guide, What Is Noom Behavior Change?, and What Does a Noom Coach Do? This page is only about the money.

The maths: what you actually save

Against the month-to-month baseline of $70 a month, the savings are large — on paper. Twelve months at $70 a month is $840; the annual plan is $209, a difference of $631. Four months at $70 a month is $280; the 4-month plan is $169, a difference of $111. Six months at $70 a month is $420; the 6-month plan is $179, a difference of $241. All three differences are arithmetic from the checked prices (checked 22 November 2026).

But every one of those savings assumes you use the whole plan. The $631 saving on the annual plan is only real if you are still using Noom in month twelve. The $111 saving on the 4-month plan is only real if you are still using it in month four. The discount is a reward for staying, not a discount on the first month — and that is the distinction the price list does not spell out.

Monthly: pay more, commit less

The month-to-month plan costs $70 a month, the highest per-month price on the list, and it is also the shortest commitment: one month, paid up front, auto-renewing. It is the plan for someone who is not sure. Two months on the monthly plan costs $140; the 2-month plan costs $129 — so even two months of monthly billing is already $11 more than buying the 2-month plan outright (arithmetic from the checked prices). Three months monthly is $210 against $159 for the 3-month plan (checked 22 November 2026).

The monthly plan makes sense in exactly one situation: you genuinely do not know whether you will still be using Noom in a month. If you are sure you will last four months, the 4-month plan is $111 cheaper. If you are sure you will last a year, the annual plan is $631 cheaper. The monthly plan is insurance against quitting — and like all insurance, it costs more than the thing it insures against.

Annual: pay least per month, commit most

The annual plan costs $209 up front, which Noom states as $17.40 a month — the lowest per-month price on the list, a quarter of the monthly plan’s $70. It is the cheapest plan on every measure except one: the amount of money that leaves your account on day one. Two hundred and nine dollars is the largest single payment on the list, paid up front when the plan starts (checked 22 November 2026).

The risk is the mirror image of the saving. If you use the full year, you save $631 against monthly billing. If you stop after two months, you have paid $209 for two months of use — against $140 for two months on the monthly plan, or $129 on the 2-month plan. Whether you get anything back if you cancel mid-plan is to be verified: Noom’s pages we read do not state a refund policy, and we do not guess one. Do not buy the annual plan on the assumption of a refund unless you have verified the policy first.

The 4-month plan: why most people pick it

Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, according to Noom’s own page — and the plan length is no accident. A Noom plan usually runs for around 16 weeks, which is about four months. The 4-month plan at $169 ($42.25 a month) roughly matches the typical program length of around 16 weeks — though the exact length varies by goal. The saving against monthly billing is $111 over the four months (checked 22 November 2026).

That is why the 4-month plan is the default answer to the question this page asks. It is not the cheapest per month — the annual plan is. It is not the most flexible — the monthly plan is. It is the plan that matches the program’s own length, at a price that sits in the middle. If you do not know which plan to pick, the 4-month plan is the one most people pick, for a reason that has nothing to do with marketing: it is the length of the program.

What to watch before you pick

Everything is prepaid and auto-renewing. All twelve plans are billed up front and renew automatically. The price you see is the price you pay at the start, and the plan renews unless you cancel it. The exact cancellation steps are to be verified — the official pages do not state them.

The trial is 7 days, and its terms are unclear. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether the trial costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified. Do not assume the trial is free.

Refunds are not stated. If you cancel mid-plan, whether you get money back is to be verified. The pages we read do not state a refund policy. This matters most for the annual plan, where $209 is paid up front.

Prices change. The figures on this page were checked on 22 November 2026. Prices on subscription pages change — sometimes without notice — so check the current price on Noom’s own pages before you buy, and treat this page’s figures as checked on the date shown, not as a live quote.

Our verdict: buy the 4-month plan unless you have a specific reason not to. It matches the program’s own length of around 16 weeks, it costs $169 up front against $280 for four months of monthly billing, and it is the plan most Noom users have. Buy the monthly plan only if you genuinely do not know whether you will last a month — and buy the annual plan only if you are sure you will use the full year, because the $631 saving is real only if you stay, and the refund policy is not stated on the pages we read. The cheapest plan on the list is the annual plan. The cheapest plan for you is the one you finish.
