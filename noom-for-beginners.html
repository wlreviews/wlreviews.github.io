Noom for Beginners

Your first week, step by step — what to do, what to ignore, and what it costs. Facts checked 22 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Start with three things: a daily 5–15 minute lesson, log everything you eat, and a coach who reads your log. Exercise starts at 200 steps a day. Plans from $70 a month, most people on the $169 4-month plan. No set meal plan, no food banned. For adults 18 and over only.

Starting a program is the hardest part, because every program looks complicated from the outside. Noom is not complicated — it is four habits, repeated daily — but the first week still has a learning curve, and most beginners waste it on the wrong things: hunting for a meal plan that does not exist, or treating the colors like a test to pass. This guide is the first week, laid out step by step, using only what Noom’s own pages say. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide describes how to start, not what starting will do for you.

What Noom is, in one paragraph

Noom is a behavior-change program built on cognitive behavioral therapy, not a diet and not a meal plan. Each day you read a short lesson — around 5–15 minutes — that teaches you why you eat the way you do, and builds new habits in small steps. You log everything you eat, and the tracker tells you whether each food is green, yellow, or orange by calorie density. A coach looks at your log, suggests different foods, and helps you stay motivated when you feel stuck. You weigh in daily if you want to — it is an option, not a requirement — and exercise starts from 200 steps a day, with food first. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks (checked 22 November 2026).

That is the whole program. Everything else on this page is detail: what the first week looks like, the five things to learn first, what you need before you start, and the three mistakes beginners make.

Your first week, day by day

The week below is assembled from the parts Noom describes — it is not one person’s diary. It shows the order the program’s parts arrive in, not a schedule anyone has to follow exactly.

  • Day 1: lesson and first log. Read the day’s lesson — around 5–15 minutes — and log what you eat, after each meal and snack or at the end of the day. The tracker tells you the color of every food you log. That is the whole of day one: one lesson, one day of logging.
  • Days 2–3: the rhythm. The same two habits, repeated: a lesson each day, and everything you eat logged. The lessons cover your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. After a few days you will start to know the colors of the foods you eat most often — and the tracker will have shown you your daily allotment for each color.
  • Days 4–5: the coach and the group. Your coach is there when you need one — you can reach out to your goal coach when you need one, and coaches adapt to your needs, checking in once every couple of weeks or more often if you need it. There is also a group of Noomers for inspiration, advice, or to share wins. Your coach works from the log you have — it doesn't need to be perfect.
  • Days 6–7: weigh-in and review. If you weigh in daily, you will have a week of readings by now — and the program’s own advice is that weight fluctuations are normal and your graph probably will not be a straight line. Look at the week the way the program asks you to: lessons done, food logged, colors learned. That is a finished first week (checked 22 November 2026).

If that sounds like a lot, it is worth saying what the work actually is: a few minutes of reading, a few taps after each meal, and a step on the scale if you want one. Noom lists “You’re busy, and don’t have a lot of time to invest in a program” among the people it is right for — and the program is built to match: short lessons, logging in the same app, exercise starting from 200 steps (checked 22 November 2026).

The five things to learn first

1. The lessons teach why, not what. The lessons are built on cognitive behavioral therapy, and they teach you why you eat the way you do — your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack — and they build new habits in small steps, with no willpower and no food restrictions. For the full explanation, see What Is Noom Behavior Change?

2. Logging means everything. Log everything you eat — yes, everything — after each meal and snack, or at the end of the day, your choice. A log that only records the good days cannot show you the pattern, and the pattern is the whole point. For the full explanation, see How Noom Food Logging Works.

3. The colors are a guide, not a test. Green, yellow, and orange by calorie density, with a suggested daily allotment for each — roughly 30% green, 45% yellow, 25% orange. No food is off limits, and the allotment is daily, not per meal. One orange-heavy lunch is not a failed day. For the full explanation, see Noom Food Color System.

4. Weigh-ins are optional. A daily weigh-in is an option, not a requirement. Daily weigh-ins help by reducing the anxiety you feel when you step on the scale, and weight fluctuations are normal. If daily weigh-ins make you anxious, the option to weigh less often is there.

5. The coach reads your log. Your coach can look at your food log and suggest different foods to try, or help you stay motivated when you feel stuck. Whether coaching is one-to-one, how quickly a coach replies, and whether you always talk to the same coach are all to be verified. For the full explanation, see What Does a Noom Coach Do?

What you need before you start

A phone with the app. The lessons, the tracker, the coach, and the group all live in the app, and the tracker syncs with Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Garmin. There is also a recipe database of thousands of recipes, which tells you how green each one is and gives you its caloric range.

Five to fifteen minutes a day. That is the lesson length. Logging takes a few taps after each meal, and exercise starts from 200 steps a day — the page's example is 200 steps, then 400, then 1,000.

A plan. No kitchen overhaul. No food is off limits, and there is no set meal plan. Noom Med is a separate program and is not covered in this guide.

Three mistakes beginners make

Skipping the log on bad days. The log’s value is the pattern, and the pattern only shows up if the bad days are in it. Noom’s own instruction is to log everything — yes, everything — and a log that only records the good days cannot show you the pattern. If a day goes off the rails, log it and move on.

Treating the colors like a test. The colors tell you how dense a food is, and the allotment tells you roughly how much of each color to aim for in a day. They are not grades, and one orange-heavy lunch is not a failed day. Green is not “good” and orange is not “bad” — orange means high calorie density and/or the least nutrition.

Expecting a meal plan. Noom does not give you a set meal plan. The recipe database is included, and the coach can suggest foods, but the program asks you to choose what to eat inside the colors and the allotment. If you want someone to tell you exactly what to eat at every meal, this program is not built for that — and it is worth knowing that before you pay.

Our verdict: Noom is a good fit for a beginner who wants structure — a short daily lesson, a log, and a coach — and a bad fit for a beginner who wants a meal plan or a tracker with no teaching. Start with the $169 4-month plan, which is roughly the length of the typical program, or the $70 monthly plan if you would rather test it month by month. The first week’s job is small: one lesson a day, log everything, and let the coach see it. If you do those three things for a week, you will know whether the program is for you — and that is the only question the first week has to answer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start Noom?

Start with the daily lesson, log everything you eat, and let your coach read your log. Exercise starts from 200 steps a day (checked 22 November 2026).

Is Noom good for beginners?

Yes, if you want structure: the program starts with lessons and a coach, and no food is off limits (checked 22 November 2026).

How much does Noom cost for beginners?

$70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription (checked 22 November 2026).

Do I need to exercise to start Noom?

No. Exercise starts from 200 steps a day, and food comes first (checked 22 November 2026).

Do I have to count calories on Noom?

The program works within a daily calorie range, and the color system helps you stay within it. The log records what you eat; it is not a calorie target you must hit each day (checked 22 November 2026).

Is there a free trial?

Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether the trial costs anything or requires a card is to be verified (checked 22 November 2026).
