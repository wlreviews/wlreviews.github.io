Noom Daily Lessons

What the lessons cover, how long they take, and what they leave out. Facts checked 23 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom gives you a short lesson every day from day one. Each takes around 5–15 minutes, is built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and gives you exercises, quizzes or tasks to complete. The lessons cover seven things Noom says you will learn — your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of snacking, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. They sit in the same app as your food log. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks. For adults 18 and over only.

Most of what people ask about Noom is food: what can I eat, what does it cost, do I have to exercise. The lessons are the part people ask about least, and the part that makes Noom different from a plain calorie counter. This guide answers the two questions that matter: what do you actually learn, and how much time does it take. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide describes the lessons, not what the lessons will do for you.

What a daily lesson is

From day 1, daily lessons of around 5–15 minutes help you change your mindset around the way you eat and feel about food. That is the figure on Noom’s main page. A second official page describes the same lessons as taking less than 15 minutes per day — the two descriptions agree, and this guide uses around 5–15 minutes as the figure.

A lesson is short by design. You get exercises, quizzes or tasks to complete, and insights into the root causes of your eating habits. The lessons are based on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), which Noom’s pages describe as the gold standard in behavioral science. The point of the lesson is not information for its own sake: it is to give you the reason behind a habit, so the change lasts after the plan ends. And the lessons live in the same app you use to log your food, so the teaching and the tracking sit side by side (checked 23 November 2026).

What you learn: seven topics

Noom’s page lists seven things you will learn. The pages do not say which day covers which topic, or in what order — so this guide lists them as themes, not as a day-by-day syllabus. Anyone telling you “week three covers plateaus” is guessing: no official page gives that sequence.

What triggers you to eat when you are not hungry. The lessons are about noticing what sets off eating when you are not hungry — the pages describe this as getting insights into the root causes of your eating habits.

How to overcome emotional and stress eating. Eating when you feel stressed, emotional, anxious, or bored is one of the patterns the lessons address directly — Noom’s own list of who the program fits includes people who want to learn what to do besides reaching for food in those moments.

What to do instead of reaching for a snack when you do not feel good. The lessons teach an alternative action for the moment you would normally snack — the replacement, not just the willpower to resist.

How to incorporate mindfulness while eating. Being in the moment and appreciating your meals, rather than eating on autopilot.

How to choose foods that pack a powerful nutrition punch but still keep you feeling full and satisfied. Choosing foods by calorie density and how filling they are, not by banning a food group — the colors are the tool for this.

How to tell the difference between feeling genuinely hungry and “head hunger”. The pages put the phrase in quotation marks, and it is worth keeping them: “head hunger” is wanting food when your body does not need it, and telling the two apart is one of the seven things the program teaches.

How to manage plateaus, when your weight loss temporarily stalls. A plateau is a stall, not a failure — the pages describe it as temporary, and managing it is part of the curriculum rather than an afterthought (checked 23 November 2026).

How a lesson fits into your day

A day on Noom has four parts, and the lesson is one of them. The pages do not prescribe what time of day to read the lesson, or in what order to do these four things — so this guide does not invent a timetable.

  • Read the day’s lesson. Around 5–15 minutes, from day 1, in the same app as your food log.
  • Log what you eat. After every meal and snack, or at the end of the day — your choice. The tracker tells you the color of everything you log, and the day’s lesson is about why you eat it.
  • Weigh in if you want to. A daily weigh-in is an option, not a requirement. Noom’s pages say daily weigh-ins help you see that small, temporary gains and losses are normal.
  • Ask your coach. Coaches adapt to your needs: check in once every couple of weeks, or more often if you are struggling. If you hit a plateau, the coach is there for that too (checked 23 November 2026).

That is the shape of a day: one lesson, food logged, the option to weigh in, and a coach you can reach. The lesson is a small part of the day by time — around 5–15 minutes — and it is the part that explains why you eat the way you do.

What the lessons do not do

They are not therapy sessions. The lessons are based on CBT, and the coaches are trained in the science behind behavior change. The pages do not describe one-to-one therapy with a clinician, and this guide does not call the lessons therapy.

They do not replace the food log, the colors, or the coach. The lesson teaches you why you eat the way you do. The log records what you actually ate. The coach looks at the log and suggests different foods to try. One without the others is a different program.

They do not give you a meal plan. The lessons teach you how to choose. The recipe database is a separate part of the program, and it is a library you pick from, not a menu you are assigned. For the full explanation, see Noom Diet Plan.

They do not come with a published syllabus. No official page lists a week-by-week plan of which topic comes when, how many lessons there are in total, or what happens to the lessons after your plan ends. Who the lessons suit, and who they do not suit

They suit you if you want structure and you are short on time. Noom’s own list of who the program fits includes people who want “a more structured program, with daily tasks, challenges and lessons to keep you on track”, and people who are “busy, and don’t have a lot of time to invest in a program”. A 5–15 minute lesson is built for that reader. They also suit you if you have tried weight-loss programs in the past and could not keep the weight off, and you want to learn what to do besides reaching for food when you feel stressed, emotional, anxious, or bored — both are on Noom’s list too. For the full explanation of the behavior-change approach, see What Is Noom Behavior Change?

They do not suit you if you only want to count calories. A tracker on its own is what MyFitnessPal sells, and it costs far less — for the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price. They also do not suit you if you want a set meal plan, or weekly group meetings in person: Noom’s list of who it fits includes people who would “rather not have to meet with a group every week”, so if weekly group meetings are the support you want, this program is not built around them (checked 23 November 2026).

What the lessons cost

The lessons are included in every plan — there is no separate lesson price on the pages. Three prices cover most buyers: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and the monthly averages on the longer plans are $17.40 a month on the annual plan, as Noom itself states, and $42.25 a month on the 4-month plan. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether the trial costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks. For the full twelve-plan table, see Noom Monthly vs Annual Plans (checked 23 November 2026).

Our verdict: Noom’s daily lessons are short, structured, and built on CBT: around 5–15 minutes a day, in the same app as your food log, covering seven topics from triggers to plateaus. They are the part of the program that teaches you why you eat the way you do — the log and the coach handle the what. If you want a program that teaches as well as tracks, the lessons are the reason to pick Noom over a plain calorie counter. If you only want to count calories, you do not need them. Start with the $169 4-month plan, which is roughly the length of the typical program, or the $70 monthly plan if you would rather test it month by month.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a Noom daily lesson?

Around 5–15 minutes a day. Noom describes the lessons as taking around 5–15 minutes, and one page describes them as taking less than 15 minutes per day (checked 23 November 2026).

What do you learn in Noom daily lessons?

Seven things: your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack, mindfulness while eating, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. The pages do not say which day covers which topic (checked 23 November 2026).

Are Noom lessons based on CBT?

Yes. Noom says the lessons are based on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), which the pages describe as the gold standard in behavioral science. The lessons are not therapy sessions with a clinician (checked 23 November 2026).

Do you get a lesson every day on Noom?

Yes. Noom describes daily lessons from day 1, in the same app you use to log your food (checked 23 November 2026).

Do the lessons replace the food log or the coach?

No. The lessons, the log, and the coach work together: the lessons teach you why you eat the way you do, the log records what you eat, and the coach can look at your log and suggest different foods to try (checked 23 November 2026).

How much do Noom daily lessons cost?

They are included in every plan: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial (checked 23 November 2026).
