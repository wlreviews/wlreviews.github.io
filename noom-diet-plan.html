Noom Diet Plan

What the plan is, what it is not, and how a day on it works. Facts checked 22 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom has no set meal plan. Instead you get a daily calorie range, a color system for every food, and a recipe database — plus a coach and daily CBT lessons. No food is off limits. Plans from $70 a month. For adults 18 and over only.

Most people searching for a “Noom diet plan” expect a list of meals. That list does not exist, and that is the first thing to understand about the program. Noom is not a diet in the usual sense — it does not tell you what to eat at breakfast, lunch, and dinner. What it gives you instead is a framework: a daily calorie range, a color for every food, a recipe database, a lesson every day, and a coach who reads your log. This guide explains that framework, what it leaves out, and how a day on it actually works. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide describes the plan, not what the plan will do for you.

What the Noom diet plan is

The plan has four parts, and they work together.

A daily calorie range. The program works within a daily calorie range, and the color system helps you stay within it. The pages we checked do not state a number for the range. What the pages do say is that the range is the boundary, and the colors are how you stay inside it.

The color system. Every food you log is green, yellow, or orange by calorie density — how many calories it packs into a given weight — with a suggested daily allotment for each: roughly 30% green, 45% yellow, and 25% orange. Green foods have the lowest calorie density, yellow foods are denser, and orange foods are the most calorie-dense and/or have the fewest nutrients. No food is off limits. For the full explanation, see Noom Food Color System.

A recipe database. A database of thousands of recipes is included, and each one tells you how green it is and gives you its caloric range. The database is a library, not a set meal plan: you pick from it, you are not assigned from it.

A coach and daily lessons. Short lessons, around 5–15 minutes a day, built on cognitive behavioral therapy, covering your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. And a coach who looks at your food log and suggests different foods to try. The lessons teach you why you eat the way you do; the coach checks what you actually ate. For the full explanation, see What Is Noom Behavior Change? and What Does a Noom Coach Do? (checked 22 November 2026).

What it is not

It is not a set meal plan. Noom does not give you a menu for the week, or tell you what to eat at each meal. If you want someone to tell you exactly what to eat at every meal, this program is not built for that — and it is worth knowing that before you pay.

It is not a list of banned foods. No food is off limits. Orange does not mean “bad” — the colors represent a food’s caloric density, and orange means high calorie density and/or the least nutrition. A plan that bans foods would be a different program.

It is not a low-carb diet, or any single-rule diet. Noom does not restrict carbohydrates or any food group. The colors are about calorie density, not about cutting out a food group. The rule is the range and the allotment, not a banned ingredient.

It is not a calorie counter on its own. The log records what you eat, and the tracker tells you the color of every food, but the program adds the lessons and the coach on top of the counting. A tracker alone is what MyFitnessPal sells — for the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price (checked 22 November 2026).

A day on the Noom plan

The day below is assembled from the parts Noom describes — it is not one person’s diary. It shows how the four parts fit together in a day.

  • Morning: lesson and first log. Read the day’s lesson — around 5–15 minutes — and log breakfast. The tracker tells you the color of everything you log, and you can see your suggested daily allotment for each color.
  • Midday: log lunch, check the colors. Log what you eat after each meal and snack, or at the end of the day — your choice. By lunch you can see how the day is shaping up against the allotment: roughly 30% green, 45% yellow, 25% orange for the day as a whole, not per meal.
  • Afternoon: a walk, if you want one. Exercise starts from 200 steps a day, and food comes first. The program’s example is 200 steps, then 400, then 1,000. For the full explanation, see Can You Use Noom Without Exercise?
  • Evening: log dinner, weigh in if you want to. Log the last meal, and weigh in if you want to — a daily weigh-in is an option, not a requirement. Your coach can look at the day’s log and suggest different foods to try (checked 22 November 2026).

That is a day on the plan: one lesson, three meals logged, colors checked against the allotment, and a coach who can see it. Nothing in the day is a meal you were told to eat.

The foods: green, yellow, orange

The color of a food is its calorie density — how many calories it packs into a given weight. Green foods have the lowest calorie density: vegetables, whole grains, and other foods that fill you up for few calories. Yellow foods are denser: lean proteins, starches, avocados, beans, and healthy fats. Orange foods are the most calorie-dense and/or have the fewest nutrients: cheeseburgers, desserts, and similar foods. The suggested daily allotment is roughly 30% green, 45% yellow, and 25% orange — and the allotment is daily, not per meal, so one orange-heavy lunch is not a failed day (checked 22 November 2026).

Two things beginners get wrong about the colors. First, they treat them as grades — green “good”, orange “bad”. The official pages say the opposite: orange, green, and yellow doesn’t mean “good” or “bad”. Second, they try to eat only green. Yellow is the largest share of the allotment: it holds the lean proteins, starches, and healthy fats that make a day’s food actually filling. A day of all green is not what the plan asks for.

Who it is for, who it is not for

It is for you if you want to choose your own food inside a framework. You pick what to eat, the colors and the allotment tell you how the day is shaping up, the lessons teach you why you eat the way you do, and the coach looks at your log. If you have tried tracking on your own and stopped, the daily lesson plus the coach plus the log is a structure that holds you in the program.

It is not for you if you want a set meal plan. The recipe database is included, and the coach can suggest foods, but the program does not give you a menu. If you want every meal decided for you, a meal-plan service is a better fit — and Noom Med is a separate program, not covered in this guide.

If you want to choose your own food and learn why you eat the way you do, the framework is the point.
