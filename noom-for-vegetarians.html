Noom for Vegetarians

Whether the color system assumes you eat meat, what it does with a vegetarian plate, and what the pages do not say. Facts checked 23 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom works the same way for vegetarians. The color system sorts food by calorie density, not by whether it comes from animals: green foods include vegetables and whole grains, yellow includes starches and healthy fats, and orange is the highest density. No food is off limits, and there is no separate vegetarian plan — the lessons, the coach, and the log are the same for everyone. The pages do not list a vegetarian meal plan, and Noom gives no separate price for vegetarians. For adults 18 and over only.

A vegetarian reading a weight-loss program has one question before any other: does this program assume I eat meat? A program built on lean chicken and fish, with vegetarian options added as an afterthought, is a different program for you than for everyone else. This guide answers that question from Noom’s own pages. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide describes how the system treats vegetarian food, not what it will do for you.

What the pages actually say

The pages do not use the word “vegetarian” in the passages we checked. What they do say is broader, and it covers you: the program works the same way whether you are vegan, following a low-carb or keto diet, or are an intermittent faster. A vegetarian diet sits inside that sentence — if the program works the same way for vegans, who eat no animal products at all, it works the same way for vegetarians. That is this guide’s reading of the page, not a quote from it, and it is the only inference this guide makes about who the program fits (checked 23 November 2026).

The reason the inference holds is the color system itself. Colors sort food by calorie density — how many calories a food has for its size — not by whether it comes from an animal. Green is the lowest calorie density, yellow is higher, orange is the highest, and orange does not mean “bad”. Nothing in that definition mentions meat. For the full explanation, see Noom Food Color System.

How the colors work for a vegetarian plate

The pages give examples for each color, not a full list. Here are the examples that belong on a vegetarian plate, taken from the pages and nothing else:

  • Green — the lowest calorie density, about 30% of what you eat. Vegetables and whole grains. These are the foods the pages say you can eat plenty of while staying within your calorie range.
  • Yellow — higher calorie density, about 45%, the largest share. Starches, avocado, and legumes, along with healthy fats. The pages list legumes and avocado as yellow examples — both vegetarian staples.
  • Orange — the highest calorie density and/or the least nutrients, about 25%. The pages’ examples are cheeseburgers and desserts. Orange does not mean “bad”, and the colors do not mean “good” or “bad” (checked 23 November 2026).

What about the foods the pages do not name — tofu, lentils, eggs, cheese on its own? The pages do not say what color they are, and this guide does not guess. That is what the tracker is for: it tells you the color of every food you log, so you learn where your own staples fall instead of taking our word for it. If a guide tells you the color of a food that no official page names, it is guessing — including this one, which is why it does not.

What does not change

The lessons are the same. The seven things you will learn — triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of snacking, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus — are the same for everyone. The pages do not describe a vegetarian version of the lessons, and this guide does not invent one.

The coach is the same. The coach can look at your food log and suggest different foods to try, and checks in once every couple of weeks, or more often if you are struggling. Whether the coach suggests vegetarian foods specifically is to be verified.

The log is the same. Log everything after each meal and snack, or at the end of the day — your choice. The tracker tells you the color of every food you log, vegetarian or not.

The price is the same. There is no separate vegetarian plan and no separate price: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year, with most Noom Weight users on the 4-month subscription. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks (checked 23 November 2026).

What the pages do not say

There is no vegetarian meal plan. Noom has no set meal plan for anyone — vegetarian or not — so there is no vegetarian version to ask for. What you get instead is a daily calorie range, the color system, a recipe database, and a coach. For the full explanation, see Noom Diet Plan.

The recipe database may or may not filter for vegetarian recipes. Noom has a database of thousands of recipes with “how green” information. The pages do not say whether you can filter it for vegetarian recipes — that is to be verified.

There is no vegetarian track. The pages describe one program, not a vegetarian edition of it. The difference for a vegetarian reader is not a different program — it is that the one program’s system does not depend on meat in the first place (checked 23 November 2026).

Who it suits, and who it does not suit

It suits you if you want a system that does not assume meat. The color system sorts by calorie density, no food is off limits, and the pages say the program works the same way for vegans — so a vegetarian plate is not an exception to the system, it is just a plate. If you already eat mostly vegetables, whole grains, and legumes, the green and yellow foods the pages name are already most of what you eat.

It does not suit you if you want a set vegetarian meal plan, or vegetarian recipes chosen for you. Noom has no set meal plan at all, and the pages do not say the recipe database filters for vegetarian recipes. If you only want to count calories, MyFitnessPal costs far less and does that job; for the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price (checked 23 November 2026).

What it costs

There is no separate price for vegetarians — the lessons, the coach, and the colors are included in every plan. Three prices cover most buyers: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and the monthly averages on the longer plans are $17.40 a month on the annual plan, as Noom itself states, and $42.25 a month on the 4-month plan. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether the trial costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks. For the full twelve-plan table, see Noom Monthly vs Annual Plans (checked 23 November 2026).

Our verdict: For a vegetarian, Noom’s system works as it works for everyone — because it sorts food by calorie density, not by whether it comes from an animal. Green is vegetables and whole grains, yellow is starches, avocado, and legumes, and nothing is off limits. There is no separate vegetarian plan, no separate price, and no set meal plan for anyone. The honest limits are two: the pages do not say whether the recipe database filters for vegetarian recipes, and they do not say what color your unlisted staples are — the tracker tells you that when you log them. If you want a system that does not assume meat, start with the $169 4-month plan, which is roughly the length of the typical program. If you want a vegetarian meal plan handed to you, Noom does not offer one.
