Noom vs MyFitnessPal: Free Trial and Features Compared

Both trials last 7 days, and they work differently. Facts checked 23 November 2026 against Noom’s and MyFitnessPal’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Both trials last 7 days, and they work differently. Noom’s 7-day initial trial starts your plan — daily CBT lessons, a coach, and the color system — and Noom calls it “no risk”, but the pages do not say whether it costs anything or requires a card. MyFitnessPal’s 7-day free trial gives first-time users Premium, and it auto-renews and charges your card when it ends unless you cancel first. Noom costs $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year; MyFitnessPal Premium costs $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year. For adults 18 and over only.

A free trial is only useful if you know what it actually gives you and what happens when it ends. These two trials look the same on the surface — 7 days, free — but one starts a whole program and the other unlocks a paid tier, and only one of them tells you in writing what happens on day 8. This guide compares the two trials and the two feature sets from the official pages, and says nothing about results — neither program promises any.

What the Noom trial gives you

The trial starts your plan, and the plan includes everything Noom sells: daily lessons built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), around 5–15 minutes from day 1, with exercises, quizzes or tasks to complete; a coach who can look at your food log and suggest different foods to try, and who checks in once every couple of weeks, or more often if you are struggling; the color system — green, yellow, orange, roughly 30/45/25, no food off limits; a log for everything you eat; a database of thousands of recipes with “how green” information; and a daily weigh-in as an option, not a requirement. The pages do not say the trial leaves anything out. What the pages also do not say is whether the trial costs anything, whether it requires a card, or what the exact cancellation steps are — all three are to be verified. For the full explanation, see The Complete Noom Guide (checked 23 November 2026).

What the MyFitnessPal trial gives you

The trial unlocks Premium for 7 days. Premium adds six things to the free account: barcode scan and meal scan; voice log — log entire meals with your voice; macro tracking with custom goals; ad-free logging; intermittent fasting; and food insights and comparison. The free account already includes nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking, so the trial does not give you tracking itself — it gives you the faster logging and the extras around it. The page describes Premium as “like having a dietitian and trainer at your fingertips” — that is marketing language, not a coaching service, and this guide does not treat it as one. The page also carries user testimonials and a survey figure; this guide does not cite either, because testimonials and survey figures are not typical results (checked 23 November 2026).

Read the table as two different tools. Noom is a teaching program with a log attached: the lessons and the coach are the product, and the log supports them. MyFitnessPal is a tracking program with a paid tier attached: the log is the product, and Premium makes logging faster (barcode, voice) and adds macro goals, fasting, and an ad-free view. Neither one is a cheaper version of the other — they do different jobs, and the trial you want is the trial of the job you want done (checked 23 November 2026).

What it costs after the trial

Noom. $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and a plan usually runs for around 16 weeks. Plans are prepaid and auto-renew.

MyFitnessPal. Premium is $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year, checked live on 22 November 2026. There is also a free account — nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking cost nothing — so after the trial you can keep using MyFitnessPal without paying, and only the Premium features stop. The Noom pages list only paid plans — no free account is described — so after the trial, the plan is paid or it ends. One caution on the MyFitnessPal price: a text crawl on 23 November 2026 showed $79.99 a year, an older cached figure; the live page checked on 22 November 2026 showed $49.99 a year and $9.99 a month. If the live page shows $79.99 again, treat the price as unstable — see the flag-note (checked 23 November 2026).

Who it suits, and who it does not suit

Try Noom’s trial first if you want to test being taught. Seven days of daily lessons and a coach will tell you whether the teaching style fits you. It will not tell you what the trial costs or how to cancel — those answers are not on the pages, so go in knowing you will have to find them yourself.

Try MyFitnessPal’s trial first if you want to test faster logging. Seven days of barcode scan, voice log, and macro goals will tell you whether Premium is worth $49.99 a year to you. Go in knowing the trial ends with a charge unless you cancel first, and that you only get one trial — if you have had Premium before, there is no second one.

Try neither if you only want to count calories for free. MyFitnessPal’s free account already does nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking without a trial and without a card. For the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price (checked 23 November 2026).

Our verdict: Both trials last 7 days, but they test different things. Noom’s trial tests a teaching program — daily CBT lessons, a coach, and the color system — and its pages do not say whether the trial costs anything, requires a card, or how to cancel. MyFitnessPal’s trial tests faster logging — barcode scan, voice log, macro goals — and its page states the terms in writing: first-time users only, auto-renews, charged unless you cancel first, and a free account to fall back on. If you want teaching, try Noom first and find the cancellation steps before day 7. If you want logging, try MyFitnessPal first and cancel before day 7 if Premium is not worth $49.99 a year to you.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Noom free trial?

7 days. Noom calls it an initial trial and says it is no risk. Whether it costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is still to be verified (checked 23 November 2026).

How long is the MyFitnessPal free trial?

7 days. It is only for people who have never upgraded to Premium and never started a Premium trial. When it ends, the first payment is charged to your card unless you cancel first (checked live 22 November 2026).

What does the MyFitnessPal trial include?

Premium for 7 days: barcode and meal scan, voice log, macro tracking with custom goals, ad-free logging, intermittent fasting, and food insights. Nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking are in the free account too (checked 23 November 2026).

What does the Noom trial include?

The trial starts your plan: daily CBT lessons, a coach, the color system, the food log, and the daily weigh-in option. The pages do not say the trial leaves anything out (checked 23 November 2026).

Can I use MyFitnessPal for free after the trial?

Yes. There is a free account with nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking. Premium features — barcode scan, voice log, macro tracking, ad-free logging — stop when the trial ends unless you pay (checked 23 November 2026).

Which trial should I try first?

Try the one whose system you want to test. Noom tests daily lessons and a coach; MyFitnessPal tests calorie counting with barcode and voice logging. Both last 7 days, and both auto-renew unless you cancel first — the Noom cancellation steps are still to be verified (checked 23 November 2026).
