WeightWatchers vs Noom: an honest comparison

Full comparison. Prices and trial terms checked 20 November 2026 against the official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article, except MyFitnessPal prices, checked live 22 November 2026. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: pick Noom if you have tried diets before, want to change how you think about food, and do not want group meetings — its daily psychology lessons and coach check-ins are built for that reader. Pick WeightWatchers if you want a points system plus live workshops and a community. Pick MyFitnessPal if you only want to track food and do not want to pay for coaching. Noom Weight costs $70 a month on a month-to-month plan, or $209 for 12 months paid up front (checked 20 November 2026). MyFitnessPal Premium costs $9.99 a month, or $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026). We promise no results and no timelines: this article compares what each program asks you to do, what it costs, and what happens when you cancel.

If you have tried diets before and always put the weight back on, the difference that matters is not the app icon — it is what the program asks you to do every day. Noom asks you to read a short lesson, log everything you eat, and check in with a coach. WeightWatchers asks you to track Points, and, on the higher plan, to show up to workshops. MyFitnessPal asks you to log food and nothing else. The rest of this article works through those differences on the same five points for each program, so you can check our work — and it names the weaknesses of the program we recommend, too.

This comparison covers the standard, non-clinical plans. It does not cover any program that involves a clinician or a prescription, and it is not medical advice. If you have a medical condition, talk to a clinician before starting a weight-loss program. For adults 18 and over only.

What Noom is

Noom Weight is a behaviour-change app built around short daily lessons on psychology, habits, and eating patterns. Noom describes it as a psychology-based approach: rather than handing you a list of permitted and forbidden foods, it works on the thoughts and feelings that shape how you eat. Its lessons are based on cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), and they cover triggers, stress and emotional eating, mindfulness, portion sizes, and telling genuine hunger apart from “head hunger”.

  • Daily lessons of around 5–15 minutes, with exercises, quizzes, or tasks (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Food is sorted into colour categories — green, yellow, orange — rather than counted as points. No food is off limits.
  • You get a personalised daily calorie range, and a plan that usually runs for around 16 weeks, longer or shorter depending on your goal (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Coaching is included: guides and coaches you can reach out to when you need support (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Price and trial terms: see “Price and free trial” below — $70 a month on a month-to-month plan, $209 for 12 months paid up front, 7-day initial trial (checked 20 November 2026).

What WeightWatchers is

WeightWatchers (WW) is a points-based program with a long-running community: workshops, meetings, and app support. Its core mechanic is the Points program: foods carry a Points value, and you work within a personalised daily budget. WW describes itself as the #1 doctor-recommended weight-loss program, based on a 2023 survey by Cerner Enviza of 500 doctors who recommend weight-loss programs (checked 20 November 2026).

  • Core: the Points program, the app to track progress, and 12,000 recipes, from $12 a month for the first 12 months on a 12-month plan — a promotional price; the plan auto-renews at the regular price, which the official pages do not state (checked 20 November 2026; offer end date not shown on official pages).
  • Core+: everything in Core, plus human coaches, expert support, and unlimited workshops (virtual and in-person), from $20 a month for the first 12 months on a 12-month plan — a promotional price; the offer ends 22 November 2026 and the plan then auto-renews at the regular price, which the official pages do not state (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Core members can attend Workshops free for the first two weeks (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Monthly prices outside the 12-month plans, and the regular Core and Core+ prices after the promotions end: to be verified.

What MyFitnessPal is, and why it is here

MyFitnessPal is a calorie and nutrient tracker, not a coaching program — a different job at a different price. It is included so readers who only want tracking can see the cheaper route instead of paying for coaching they will not use.

  • Free tier: nutrition and weight tracking and exercise tracking (checked 20 November 2026).
  • Premium adds barcode scanning, Meal Scan, voice logging, custom macro goals, an ad-free experience, and priority support. Premium costs $9.99 a month, billed monthly, or $49.99 a year, billed annually (checked 22 November 2026).
  • Premium+ (a custom meal planner, 1,500+ recipes, and automated grocery lists) was listed at $99.99 billed annually or $24.99 billed monthly when checked 20 November 2026, but was not on the official Premium page when re-checked live on 22 November 2026.
  • There is a 7-day free trial (checked 22 November 2026). The trial auto-renews unless you cancel before it ends, and the first charge lands on your card at the end of the trial.
  • No lessons, no coach, no workshops, no community program — just tracking.

What a typical day looks like

Noom

Around 5–15 minutes of lessons a day — Noom describes its daily lessons as taking less than 15 minutes per day. Log everything you eat, in the app, after each meal or at the end of the day — your choice. Optionally weigh in daily; Noom says daily weigh-ins are optional and that small day-to-day fluctuations are normal. Check in with your coach as often or as rarely as you like — once every couple of weeks is fine, or more often if you are struggling. Exercise is not required to start: Noom says it starts with food, and suggests starting with 200 steps a day when you are ready, building up as your confidence builds (checked 20 November 2026).

WeightWatchers

Track what you eat against your Points budget in the app, using the recipe library when you need ideas. On Core+, attend a workshop — virtual or in person — and talk to a coach; on Core, use the app and the Connect community instead. Core members get their first two weeks of Workshops free, so the workshop habit is something you can try before deciding whether the higher plan is worth it (checked 20 November 2026). The exact daily time commitment is not stated on the official plans page, so it is to be verified.

MyFitnessPal

Log meals and activity; nothing else is asked of you. No lessons, no coach, no meetings. Premium adds faster logging — barcode scan, Meal Scan, voice logging — and custom macro goals. If you want structure handed to you each day, this is the wrong app; if you want the fastest possible log, it is the most direct one.

Coaching and support

Noom: a coach and guides are included in the program. Noom says its coaches are trained in the science behind behaviour change, adapt to how often you want to check in, can look at your food log and suggest different foods, and can give more intensive help when you hit a plateau (checked 20 November 2026). There is also a community of other members, available around the clock. How quickly a coach replies, and whether coaching is one-to-one messaging or group-based, is to be verified.

WeightWatchers: human coaches and expert support are the headline difference between Core+ and Core, along with unlimited workshops (checked 20 November 2026). Core members get the Connect community and the app. Access to Registered Dietitians is listed on the plans page, but the page’s comparison table does not make clear in text which plans include it, so that detail is to be verified.

MyFitnessPal: no coaching. Premium includes priority customer support, which is help with the app, not a health coach (checked 20 November 2026).

Food tracking: colours, Points, or calories

Noom — green, yellow, orange. Every food you log is categorised green, yellow, or orange by calorie density: how much of it you would need to eat to feel satisfied. Foods land in green when they pack the fewest calories for their bulk — most vegetables, some fruits, and whole grains sit here. Yellow covers the middle ground — lean meats, starches, and healthy fats such as avocados and legumes. Orange holds the foods with the most calories per bite, or the fewest nutrients — cheeseburgers and desserts are Noom’s examples here. Noom suggests roughly 30% of daily intake from green foods, 45% from yellow, and 25% from orange (checked 20 November 2026). Orange does not mean “bad”, and no food is off limits — the colours are a guide to portions, not a ban list. Note: some older articles call the third colour red. Noom’s current official pages call it orange, and this article follows the current official wording.

WeightWatchers — Points. Each food carries a Points value and you work within a personalised daily budget. It is the most structured of the three systems: the number does the deciding for you, which suits readers who want a clear daily limit and do not want to think about calorie density. The exact list of zero-Point foods and how the budget is calculated for each person are not stated on the plans page, so they are to be verified.

MyFitnessPal — calories and macros. You log food against a calorie target, with macro goals (protein, carbs, fat) customisable on Premium. Premium adds barcode scanning, Meal Scan, and voice logging. It teaches you nothing about why you eat. It is tracking, not a program.

Who each program may suit — and who it may not

Noom may suit you if…

  • You have tried diets before and put the weight back on, and you want a different starting point — Noom’s own list of who it is for starts with exactly that reader (checked 20 November 2026).
  • You want to understand why you eat — triggers, stress, boredom — rather than just count what you eat.
  • You would rather not meet with a group every week (checked 20 November 2026).
  • You are busy and have 5–15 minutes a day for a lesson, but not time for complicated meal prep or meetings.
  • You eat out often: you can eat your favourite foods, including restaurant meals — you log them and stay within your calorie range, and the community can help with specific choices (checked 20 November 2026).
  • You are vegetarian or follow another way of eating: no food is off limits and Noom says the program fits every lifestyle, with your coach helping you fit it to how you eat (checked 20 November 2026).

Noom may not suit you if: you dislike logging everything you eat, every day — logging is a core part of the program, not optional. Or if the up-front payment puts you off: $169 for four months or $209 for a year is charged in one go. Or if you want face-to-face group support, which Noom does not offer.

WeightWatchers may suit you if…

  • You want structure handed to you as a number — a Points budget — rather than lessons about psychology.
  • You want live workshops and a community, in person or virtual.
  • You want the cheapest entry point of the two paid programs: Core from $12 a month for the first 12 months on a 12-month plan — a promotional price; the plan then auto-renews at the regular price, which the official pages do not state, and no offer end date is shown for Core (checked 20 November 2026).

WeightWatchers may not suit you if: you do not want group meetings, or you do not want a 12-month commitment to get the lowest monthly rate. The month-to-month price outside the 12-month plan is to be verified.

MyFitnessPal may suit you if…

  • You only want tracking — calories, macros, exercise — and you already know how to eat and just want the numbers.
  • You want a free tier, or the cheapest paid tier: Premium is $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026).

MyFitnessPal may not suit you if: you need lessons, a coach, or a community to keep going. It provides none of those, and the free tier shows ads and leaves barcode scanning and custom macro goals behind the paywall.

Strengths and weaknesses

Noom

Strengths: the only one of the three built around changing how you think about food, with daily CBT-based lessons; a coach is included rather than sold as an upgrade; no food is banned; lessons take only 5–15 minutes a day; you can eat out and follow any way of eating.

Weaknesses: the most expensive headline price — $70 a month if you pay month to month — and multi-month plans are charged in full up front, which is a real commitment before you know whether you will stick with it. Daily logging of everything you eat is a chore, and the program is only as good as the logging you actually do. The 7-day trial is short: a week is enough to try the lessons, not to judge a habit. Coaching reply times and whether the coach is one-to-one are still to be verified.

WeightWatchers

Strengths: the cheapest entry point of the two paid programs, a simple Points system that needs no psychology lessons to understand, live workshops and a community that has run for decades, and a large recipe library (12,000 recipes, checked 20 November 2026).

Weaknesses: the lowest prices require a 12-month plan, and the Core+ promotional price ends 22 November 2026, after which the regular price is not stated on the plans page. If you do not want workshops, the Core plan gives you little that a tracker does not. The program’s own evidence claims are quoted on its site, but this article does not repeat outcome figures — they are the company’s claims, not our verification.

MyFitnessPal

Strengths: the cheapest paid option, a free tier that covers basic tracking, and the fastest logging tools of the three (barcode, Meal Scan, voice). No lessons to keep up with and no meetings to attend.

Weaknesses: no coach, no lessons, no community program — if you have quit trackers before, nothing here addresses why. Barcode scanning, custom macro goals, and an ad-free experience all sit behind the Premium paywall, and Premium costs $9.99 a month if you pay monthly rather than $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026).

Common objections, answered

“I don’t like logging food.” Then be honest with yourself: all three programs ask you to log. Noom asks you to log everything you eat; WeightWatchers asks you to track Points; MyFitnessPal asks you to log calories. The lightest version is MyFitnessPal’s free tier — logging only, no lessons, no meetings. If logging itself is the problem, none of these three will fix it.

“I don’t want group meetings.” Noom and MyFitnessPal are both app-only; Noom explicitly lists “you’d rather not have to meet with a group every week” as a reason it may fit you (checked 20 November 2026). WeightWatchers’ Core plan is app-based too, but its workshops are the point of Core+.

“I’ve tried apps before and quit.” That is the reader Noom is written for — its own description starts with people who have tried weight-loss programs and could not keep the weight off (checked 20 November 2026). What is different is the structure: daily lessons about triggers and stress eating, plus a coach, rather than a bare calorie counter. What is not different is the daily work: you still log everything, and a week’s trial will not tell you whether you will still be logging in month three.

“Is the free trial really free?” Noom’s official page describes a 7-day initial trial and does not state a trial fee — whether a fee applies is to be verified. MyFitnessPal’s official page offers a 7-day free trial (checked 22 November 2026); the trial auto-renews unless you cancel before it ends, and the first charge lands on your card at the end of the trial. WeightWatchers lists no free trial of the full plan, only two free weeks of Workshops on Core (checked 20 November 2026). In every case, check the trial end date on the day you sign up: Noom’s plans auto-renew, MyFitnessPal’s trial auto-renews unless cancelled, and WeightWatchers’ 12-month plans auto-renew unless cancelled.

“What does it really cost, and what am I locked into?” Noom: $70 a month, or $169 for four months / $209 for twelve months paid up front, auto-renewing. WeightWatchers: Core from $12 a month for the first 12 months (promotional price; then renews at the undisclosed regular price; offer end date not shown on official pages), Core+ from $20 a month for the first 12 months (offer ends 22 November 2026), both on 12-month plans that auto-renew. MyFitnessPal: Premium $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026); Premium+ ($99.99 a year or $24.99 a month, checked 20 November 2026) was not on the official page on 22 November 2026. Noom and WeightWatchers prices checked 20 November 2026. The lock-in is longest at Noom (lump-sum multi-month payment) and WeightWatchers (12-month plan for the lowest rate); MyFitnessPal’s annual plan is the smallest lump sum of the three.

“Is this a real programme or a fad?” Noom is a behaviour-change program built on CBT-based lessons, not a diet that cuts out food groups; WeightWatchers is a long-running program that cites a 2023 survey of 500 doctors (checked 20 November 2026). Neither claim is a promise about your outcome — treat brand history as trust evidence, not as a guarantee.

Our recommendation

Our pick for the reader this article is written for — someone who has tried diets before, wants to change how they think about food, and does not want group meetings — is Noom. The daily lessons and included coach match that reader’s problem more directly than a Points budget or a bare tracker, and the 5–15 minute daily commitment fits a busy schedule. Go in with your eyes open about its weaknesses: $70 a month is the highest headline price here, the cheaper rates mean paying $169 or $209 in one go, the 7-day trial is short, and the whole program depends on you logging everything, every day.

Choose WeightWatchers instead if you want a simple daily number and live human contact — workshops and a community — and you are comfortable with a 12-month plan to get Core from $12 a month for the first 12 months (promotional price; then renews at the undisclosed regular price; offer end date not shown on official pages) or Core+ from $20 a month for the first 12 months (checked 20 November 2026; Core+ promotional price ends 22 November 2026).

Choose MyFitnessPal instead if you only want tracking and you do not want to pay for lessons or coaching: the free tier costs nothing, and Premium is $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026).

If the checks had not supported a clear pick, we would have said so rather than force one. They support a conditional pick: Noom for the behaviour-change reader, WeightWatchers for the structure-and-community reader, MyFitnessPal for the tracking-only reader.

No. Noom says it starts with food when it comes to weight loss, and suggests starting with 200 steps a day when you are ready, building up as your confidence builds. Exercise tracking is included, but exercise is not a requirement to join or use the program (checked 20 November 2026).

The annual plan. Which is better, Noom or WeightWatchers?

It depends on fit. WeightWatchers is built around its Points system and workshops; Noom is built around psychology-based lessons and food colour categories. Choose Noom if you want lessons and a coach without group meetings; choose WeightWatchers if you want Points and a live community. Neither suits everyone.

How does Noom work?

You answer questions about your weight, goals, and dieting history (about 10 minutes), then get a plan — usually around 16 weeks — with a daily calorie range. From day one you get daily lessons of 5–15 minutes, log everything you eat, optionally weigh in daily, and can reach out to a coach (checked 20 November 2026).

What is Noom’s food colour system?

Green, yellow, and orange, by calorie density. Green holds the foods with the fewest calories for their bulk — most vegetables, some fruits, and whole grains. Yellow covers the middle ground — lean meats, starches, and healthy fats. Orange holds the most calorie-dense foods — cheeseburgers and desserts are Noom’s examples. Noom suggests about 30% green, 45% yellow, 25% orange. No food is off limits (checked 20 November 2026).

What are Noom’s daily lessons?

Short lessons of around 5–15 minutes, with exercises, quizzes, or tasks, based on CBT. They cover triggers, stress and emotional eating, mindfulness, portion sizes, and hunger cues (checked 20 November 2026).

What does a Noom coach do?

Your coach supports you with tips and encouragement, can look at your food log and suggest different foods, and can give more intensive help when you hit a plateau. Coaches adapt to how often you want to check in — once every couple of weeks is fine. They are trained in behaviour-change science (checked 20 November 2026).

Can you use Noom without exercising?

Yes. Noom starts with food and treats exercise as something to build in when you are ready, starting with 200 steps a day. The app includes a pedometer and exercise tracking, and syncs with Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Garmin — but none of that is required (checked 20 November 2026).

Can vegetarians use Noom?

Yes. No food is off limits, and Noom says the program fits every lifestyle — your coach can help you fit it to the way you eat (checked 20 November 2026). The colour system works by calorie density, not by food group, so it does not depend on eating meat. Note: the official pages do not name vegetarian plans specifically; this is our reading of their general statement.

Can you eat out on Noom?

Yes. You log restaurant meals like any other food and aim to stay within your calorie range, and the community can help with specific choices — Noom’s own example is asking other members what works when you want Chinese food (checked 20 November 2026).

Does Noom help with emotional eating?

That is what the lessons are for: they cover what triggers you to eat when you are not hungry, how to overcome emotional and stress eating, and what to do instead of reaching for a snack when you do not feel good (checked 20 November 2026).

Noom vs MyFitnessPal for a busy schedule?

Noom asks for 5–15 minutes of lessons a day plus logging; MyFitnessPal asks only for logging, with faster tools (barcode, Meal Scan, voice). If your day has no room for lessons, MyFitnessPal is the lighter daily load. If you want the lessons, Noom describes them as taking less than 15 minutes a day (checked 20 November 2026).
