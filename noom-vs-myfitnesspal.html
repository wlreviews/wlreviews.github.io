Noom vs MyFitnessPal: Behavior Change or Calorie Counting?

A head-to-head comparison. Prices and trial terms checked 22 November 2026 against the official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. MyFitnessPal’s prices changed between checks — see the note at the end. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom and MyFitnessPal solve different problems. Noom is a behaviour-change program: daily psychology lessons, a colour-coded food system, and a goal coach, for $70 a month or $209 a year paid up front (checked 22 November 2026). MyFitnessPal is a calorie-counting tracker: log food with barcode or voice search, track macros, and log exercise, with a free version and Premium at $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026). If you want to change why you eat, Noom teaches; if you want to record what you eat, MyFitnessPal counts. Neither promises results, and neither is medical advice. For adults 18 and over only.

This comparison uses the same five points for both apps — method, food tracking, coaching, price, and trial — so the differences are visible instead of asserted. The two apps are often put side by side as if they were two versions of the same thing. They are not. One is a course with a coach; the other is a logbook with a barcode scanner. The right choice depends on which of those two things you will actually use, and the wrong choice is usually the more expensive one bought for the features you never touch.

WeightWatchers is the third app in our comparison set. It is not compared in detail here — the three-way comparison is WeightWatchers vs Noom: an honest comparison, and the full Noom guide is The Complete Noom Guide. This article stays on Noom against MyFitnessPal.

The core difference: behaviour change vs calorie counting

Noom’s own description is that it is a weight-loss program that helps you change your habits and mindset around food. The method is psychology first: daily lessons based on cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), which Noom describes as a type of therapy proven to help people change habits and behaviour. The lessons cover what triggers you to eat when you are not hungry, how to overcome emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack when you do not feel good, and how to tell genuine hunger apart from “head hunger” (checked 22 November 2026).

MyFitnessPal’s method is the opposite order. It does not teach you why you eat; it records what you eat. You log food, it counts the calories and macros, and it shows you the totals against your goal. Its Premium features are all tracking tools: barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking, meal scan, food insights and comparison, intermittent fasting, and an ad-free experience (checked 22 November 2026). There is no lesson and no coach anywhere in the list.

That is the whole decision, stated plainly. If your problem is that you eat for reasons you do not understand — stress, boredom, habit — a logbook will show you the numbers without touching the reasons. If your problem is that you simply do not know how much you are eating, lessons about triggers will not help as fast as a barcode scan. Most people who have tried a tracker and quit did not quit because the tracking was wrong; they quit because nothing in the app addressed why they stopped logging. Noom is built for that reader. MyFitnessPal is built for the reader who will keep logging without being taught.

How Noom works

You start by answering questions on Noom’s website about your current weight, your goal weight, and your experience with dieting and weight loss. It takes about 10 minutes. You then receive a plan — usually for around 16 weeks, longer or shorter depending on your goal — with a suggested daily calorie range (checked 22 November 2026).

From day one, you get a daily lesson of around 5–15 minutes, with exercises, quizzes, or tasks. You log everything you eat in the app, after each meal or at the end of the day — your choice — and you can weigh in daily if you want to. A coach and guides are included, and there is a community of other members available around the clock. No food is off limits: you log it and aim to stay within your calorie range (checked 22 November 2026).

The full detail — the 12 plan prices, the food colour system, coaching, exercise, and cancelling — is in The Complete Noom Guide. What matters for this comparison is the shape of the program: a short daily lesson, logging, an optional weigh-in, and a coach you can message. The daily ask is small, but it is daily, and the program only works if the small ask actually happens.

How MyFitnessPal works

MyFitnessPal is a tracker, and it is honest about that: it calls itself the number one nutrition tracking app. You log what you eat — by searching for foods, scanning barcodes, or logging whole meals with your voice — and the app counts calories and macros against the goals you set. Exercise tracking is included, and the free version covers basic food and exercise tracking (checked 22 November 2026).

Premium adds the tools that make logging faster and more detailed: barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking with your own goals, an ad-free experience, meal scan, intermittent fasting, and food insights that compare foods. MyFitnessPal’s own pitch for Premium is time saved and detail gained, not behaviour changed: “Save time, scan the barcode”, “Log entire meals with your voice”, “Enjoy an ad-free logging experience” (checked 22 November 2026).

There is no program to follow and no lesson to read. You set your own calorie and macro goals, you log, and the app shows you where you stand. That is a strength if you know what you are doing and want a fast logbook, and a weakness if you want someone to tell you what to change. MyFitnessPal does not pretend to be anything else — the whole Premium page is about tracking, and nothing on it offers coaching or lessons.

Food tracking compared

Both apps ask you to log everything you eat. What they do with the log is different. Noom categorises every food green, yellow, or orange by calorie density — how much of it you would need to eat to feel satisfied — and suggests roughly 30% green, 45% yellow, and 25% orange a day. The colours are a guide to portions, not a ban list: no food is off limits (checked 22 November 2026).

MyFitnessPal categorises by numbers instead of colours. The log shows calories and macros — protein, carbs, fat — against the custom goals you set, and Premium adds food insights that compare foods. The barcode scanner and voice log are the speed advantage: logging a packaged food or a whole meal takes seconds, where Noom’s logging is manual entry plus a colour judgement (checked 22 November 2026).

Which tracking is better depends on what you get wrong. If you get portions wrong — eating a lot of calorie-dense food without noticing — Noom’s colour system makes the pattern visible in a way a calorie total does not. If you get macros wrong — hitting your calories but missing your protein, for example — MyFitnessPal’s custom macro tracking shows it directly, and Noom’s colours will not. Neither app checks whether what you logged is what you actually ate; both are only as good as the logging you actually do.

Coaching and support

Noom includes a goal coach. Noom says its coaches are trained in the science behind behaviour change, adapt to how often you want to check in — once every couple of weeks is fine, or more often if you are struggling — can look at your food log and suggest different foods, and can give more intensive help when you hit a plateau. There is also a community of other members, available around the clock (checked 22 November 2026).

MyFitnessPal offers no coach and no lessons. Support, in MyFitnessPal’s case, is the app itself: the log, the goals, and the insights. That is not a flaw in a tracker — a tracker is not supposed to coach — but it is the clearest line between the two apps. If you want a person to talk to when you stall, only one of them provides one.

Two things about Noom’s coaching we could not verify, and so we do not state them as facts: how quickly a coach replies, and whether coaching is one-to-one messaging or group-based. Both are to be verified. If the speed and format of coaching matter to your decision, check them on Noom’s official help pages or by asking during the trial — do not assume a coach is on call.

Every plan is auto-renewing, and every plan starts with an initial 7-day trial (checked 22 November 2026). MyFitnessPal Premium is simpler: an annual plan or a monthly plan (checked 22 November 2026). The price gap buys something specific: lessons and a coach. If you will read the daily lessons and talk to the coach, the $209 a year buys something a tracker does not offer. If you only want to log food, a tracker costs less — and the free version of MyFitnessPal costs nothing at all. Paying Noom’s price for MyFitnessPal’s job is the most expensive mistake in this comparison.

Free trials compared

Both apps offer 7 days free — but the two trials are not the same thing. Noom’s plans all start with an initial 7-day trial (checked 22 November 2026). What Noom’s official page does not state is whether the trial itself carries a fee — that is to be verified, and it is not printed here as a fact. Noom’s plans auto-renew, so a trial left alone becomes a paid plan: note the trial end date on the day you sign up, and check what plan the trial converts into.

MyFitnessPal offers a 7-day free trial on its official Premium page (checked 22 November 2026). The page’s own Q&A states the terms plainly: the first Premium subscription charge lands on your credit card at the end of the trial unless you cancel before then, and once the trial ends, the subscription auto-renews unless you cancel — you cancel in the Settings menu, in the App Store, or in Google Play, depending on where you signed up. So unlike Noom, whose trial fee is still unverified, MyFitnessPal’s trial mechanics are confirmed: the trial is free to start, but it converts to a paid plan unless you act before it ends. Note the trial end date on the day you sign up.

Seven days is short for either app. A week is enough to try Noom’s lessons and MyFitnessPal’s logging, not to judge a habit. Treat the trial as a test of whether you will do the daily work — the lesson, the logging — not as a test of whether the app works.

Who each one suits — and who it does not

Noom may suit you if…

  • You have tried trackers or diets before and could not keep the weight off, and you want a different starting point — Noom’s own list starts with exactly that reader (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You want to understand why you eat — triggers, stress, boredom — rather than just count what you eat.
  • You would rather not meet with a group every week (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You want a structured program with daily tasks, challenges, and lessons to keep you on track, and clear steps to follow every day rather than being left on your own (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You are busy and have 5–15 minutes a day for a lesson, but not time for complicated meal prep or meetings.

MyFitnessPal may suit you if…

  • You already know roughly what to eat and only want to see the numbers — calories and macros against your own goals.
  • You want the cheapest way to log food: the free version does the job, and Premium is $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You care about macros, not just calories: custom macro tracking with your own goals is a Premium feature (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You want logging to be fast: barcode scanning and voice logging are the point of Premium (checked 22 November 2026).
  • You do not want lessons, a coach, or a program — just a logbook that counts.

Noom may not suit you if: you dislike logging everything you eat, every day — logging is a core part of the program, not optional. Or if the up-front payment puts you off: $169 for four months or $209 for a year is charged in one go. Or if you want a meal plan handed to you — Noom gives you a calorie range and colour guidance, not a menu.

MyFitnessPal may not suit you if: you have tried trackers before and quit, and the reason you quit was not the tracking but the lack of structure — no lessons, no coach, nothing to read when motivation drops. Or if you want someone to tell you what to change: the app shows you the numbers and leaves the decisions to you.

Strengths and weaknesses

Noom — strengths: the only one of the two built around changing how you think about food, with daily CBT-based lessons; a coach is included rather than sold as an upgrade; no food is banned; lessons take only 5–15 minutes a day; the community is available around the clock.

Noom — weaknesses: the most expensive headline price — $70 a month if you pay month to month — and multi-month plans are charged in full up front, which is a real commitment before you know whether you will stick with it. Daily logging of everything you eat is a chore, and the program is only as good as the logging you actually do. The 7-day trial is short, and the trial fee is still to be verified.

MyFitnessPal — strengths: the cheapest way to log food — the free version costs nothing, and Premium is $49.99 a year; barcode and voice logging make the daily log fast; custom macro tracking for people who care about protein, carbs, and fat, not just calories; no lessons and no coach means nothing to keep up with except the log itself.

MyFitnessPal — weaknesses: no lessons and no coach, so nothing in the app addresses why you eat or why you stop logging; the free version is basic — barcode and voice logging, custom macros, and the ad-free experience are all behind Premium; the app shows you numbers and leaves every decision to you.

Common objections, answered

“I’ve tried a tracker before and quit.” Then ask why you quit. If you quit because logging was slow, MyFitnessPal Premium’s barcode and voice logging fix that. If you quit because nothing in the app kept you going, a faster logbook will not fix it — that is the reader Noom is written for, with daily lessons and a coach (checked 22 November 2026).

“I don’t want to pay $70 a month.” Then do not start with Noom’s month-to-month plan. MyFitnessPal’s free version costs nothing, and Premium is $49.99 a year — about $4.17 a month. The question is whether the free tracker gives you what you need; if it does, Noom’s price buys features you will not use.

“Is the free trial really free?” MyFitnessPal’s 7-day free trial is free to start, but the first charge lands on your card at the end of the trial unless you cancel before then, and the subscription auto-renews (checked 22 November 2026). Noom’s 7-day initial trial is described on its official page without stating a trial fee — whether a fee applies is to be verified. In both cases, note the trial end date on the day you sign up: both plans auto-renew.

“What does it really cost, and what am I locked into?” Noom: $70 a month, or $169 for four months / $209 for twelve months paid up front, auto-renewing (checked 22 November 2026). MyFitnessPal Premium: $49.99 a year or $9.99 a month (checked 22 November 2026). Noom’s lock-in is the lump-sum payment; MyFitnessPal’s free version has no lock-in at all.

“Can I just use both?” You can, but you would be paying twice for the same log. Noom already includes food logging, and MyFitnessPal’s free version logs food for nothing. If you want Noom’s lessons and coach, use Noom and skip Premium; if you want only a log, use MyFitnessPal and skip Noom. Paying for both buys one log twice.

Our verdict

Our verdict: choose by the problem, not by the price tag. If you want to change why you eat, Noom is the only one of the two that teaches — daily lessons and a coach for $70 a month or $209 a year. If you want to record what you eat, MyFitnessPal is the better tool — the free version logs food for nothing, and Premium is $49.99 a year. The most common mistake is buying Noom for MyFitnessPal’s job: paying $70 a month for a log you could keep free. The second most common mistake is the reverse: buying a tracker when what you needed was a teacher, and quitting for the same reason you quit last time.

Do not choose Noom if you only want tracking — a tracker costs less and asks less of you. Do not choose MyFitnessPal if you have already tried trackers and quit because nothing kept you going. Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Noom or MyFitnessPal?

It depends on the problem you want solved. If you want to change why you eat, Noom teaches behaviour change with daily lessons and a coach. If you want to record what you eat, MyFitnessPal counts calories and macros with a tracker. Neither is better in general — they are different tools.

Is MyFitnessPal free?

Yes, a free version exists with basic food and exercise tracking. Premium adds barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking, an ad-free experience, meal scan, and more. Premium costs $49.99 a year; the Monthly plan is $9.99 a month. A 7-day free trial is offered (all checked 22 November 2026).

How much does Noom cost compared to MyFitnessPal?

Noom costs $70 a month on a month-to-month plan, or $209 for 12 months paid up front. MyFitnessPal Premium costs $49.99 a year, or $9.99 a month. Over a year, Noom costs about 4.2 times as much as MyFitnessPal Premium ($209 against $49.99). All prices checked 22 November 2026.

Does MyFitnessPal have a coach?

No. MyFitnessPal is a tracker: its Premium features are tracking tools — barcode and voice logging, custom macros, meal scan, food insights. It offers no coach and no daily lessons. Noom includes a goal coach who adapts to how often you want to check in (checked 22 November 2026).

Can I use MyFitnessPal without paying?

Yes. The free version covers food and exercise tracking. You pay only if you want the Premium tools: barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking, an ad-free experience, meal scan, intermittent fasting, and food insights (checked 22 November 2026).

Do I have to exercise to use either app?

No, neither app requires exercise. Noom says it starts with food when it comes to weight loss, and suggests starting with 200 steps a day when you are ready. MyFitnessPal includes exercise tracking, but tracking is optional — you can log food only (both checked 22 November 2026).

Which app is better for beginners?

If you have never tracked food before and only want to see what you eat, MyFitnessPal is the lighter start: the free version does the job, and Premium is $49.99 a year. If you have tried trackers before and quit, Noom is built for that reader — daily lessons and a coach — but it costs $70 a month and asks you to log everything, every day (both checked 22 November 2026).

Can I switch from MyFitnessPal to Noom?

Yes. Nothing locks you into either app. Try the free version of MyFitnessPal first if you only want tracking; if you find you need lessons and a coach, try Noom’s 7-day initial trial — but note the trial fee is still to be verified, and Noom’s plans auto-renew, so check the trial end date on the day you sign up (checked 22 November 2026).
