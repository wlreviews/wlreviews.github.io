Noom for People Who Have Tried Diets Before

What Noom does differently from the diets you have already tried, what it does the same, and what the pages do not say. Facts checked 23 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom’s answer to past diets is not another set of rules. It is a different approach: daily lessons built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), a coach, and a color system for food — no food off limits, no willpower, no set meal plan. The lessons teach you the why behind how you eat: triggers, emotional and stress eating, and what to do instead of snacking. Noom says the program usually runs for around 16 weeks, and the pages give no outcome figures for people who have tried diets before. For adults 18 and over only.

If you have tried diets before, you already know what a diet looks like: a list of rules, a list of banned foods, and a plan that works until it does not. The question this guide answers is whether Noom is another version of that, or something else. Noom’s own list of who the program fits starts with people who have tried weight-loss programs in the past but have not been able to keep the weight off — so this guide is written for the reader the pages themselves name first. It covers Noom Weight only. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide describes what is different, not whether the difference will work for you.

What “tried diets before” means on Noom’s pages

Noom’s pages name several kinds of reader who fit the program. Three of them will sound familiar if you have dieted before. People who have tried weight-loss programs in the past but have not been able to keep the weight off. People who want a different approach — one that is sustainable, rather than another set of rules. And people who want a more structured program, with daily tasks, challenges and lessons to keep them on track. A second official page adds that the program aims to help you sustain the healthy habits you develop and avoid yo-yo dieting. If that is you — the rules worked for a while, then stopped working — the pages are written for you (checked 23 November 2026).

Notice what the pages do not do: they do not say why your past diets stopped working, and they do not promise that this one will be different. The claim on the pages is narrower than that: the approach is different. The rest of this guide is about what that difference consists of, and where it ends.

What is different

The lessons teach the why, not the what. A diet tells you what to eat. Noom’s daily lessons, built on CBT, teach you why you eat the way you do: what triggers you to eat when you are not hungry, how to overcome emotional and stress eating, and what to do instead of reaching for a snack when you do not feel good. The lessons take around 5–15 minutes a day, start on day 1, and give you exercises, quizzes or tasks to complete. For the full explanation, see What Is Noom Behavior Change? and Noom Daily Lessons.

No food is off limits. The color system sorts food by calorie density — green, yellow, orange, roughly 30/45/25 — and no food is banned. That is the opposite of a diet built on a forbidden list, and it is a point the pages make more than once: no willpower, no food restrictions. For the full explanation, see Noom Food Color System.

No set meal plan. Noom does not give you a menu to follow. Instead you get a daily calorie range, the color system, a recipe database, and a coach — and you choose what to eat inside that frame. For the full explanation, see Noom Diet Plan.

A coach who looks at your log. The coach can look at your food log and suggest different foods to try, and is there when you hit a plateau and do not know what to do. A set of rules leaves you to follow it on your own; Noom’s pages describe someone checking in once every couple of weeks, or more often if you are struggling. For the full explanation, see What Does a Noom Coach Do? (checked 23 November 2026).

What is the same

It is still a weight-loss program, and it still asks for daily work. The lesson comes every day, the log records everything you eat — yes, everything — and a daily weigh-in is an option, not a requirement. If your past diets failed because you stopped doing them, Noom asks for the same daily habit, in a different form.

It still takes about the same time. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks, and the pages do not describe a separate schedule for people who have tried diets before. There is no fast track for repeat dieters, and no shortcut version of the program.

It still costs the same. The lessons, the coach, and the colors are included in every plan: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year, with most Noom Weight users on the 4-month subscription. There is no cheaper plan for people who have already paid for other programs (checked 23 November 2026).

What the pages do not say

There are no outcome figures for people who have tried diets before. The pages give no numbers for how repeat dieters do on Noom, and this guide does not quote the study figures from Noom’s pages as a promise for anyone — repeat dieter or not. We do not use them here.

There is no comparison of success rates. No official page compares Noom’s results with other programs, or says it works better for people who have tried diets before than for people who have not. The pages say the approach is different; they do not say the results are better.

There is no separate track. The pages do not describe different lessons, a different coach, or a different plan for people who have tried diets before. The seven topics, the daily lesson, and the coach are the same for everyone — the difference is the approach, not a separate version of the program (checked 23 November 2026).

Who it suits, and who it does not suit

It suits you if the rules were the problem. If past diets worked while you followed the rules and stopped working when the rules ran out, Noom’s answer is to teach the why instead of handing you another set of rules: triggers, emotional eating, and what to do instead of snacking, in daily lessons of around 5–15 minutes. It also suits you if you want structure — daily tasks, challenges and lessons to keep you on track — rather than a plan you follow alone. For the emotional eating angle in full, see Noom for Emotional Eaters.

It does not suit you if you want a set meal plan, or a quick fix. Noom has no set meal plan, and the pages do not describe it as a quick fix: a plan usually runs for around 16 weeks, built on daily lessons. If you only want to count calories, MyFitnessPal costs far less and does that job; for the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price (checked 23 November 2026).

What it costs

Three prices cover most buyers: $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and the monthly averages on the longer plans are $17.40 a month on the annual plan, as Noom itself states, and $42.25 a month on the 4-month plan. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether the trial costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified. A plan usually runs for around 16 weeks. Different: it teaches the why — triggers, emotional eating, and what to do instead of snacking — with no food off limits and no set meal plan. The same: it asks for daily work, it takes around 16 weeks, and it costs the same as it costs everyone else. The pages give no outcome figures for repeat dieters, and this guide promises none.
