Is Noom Worth It?

An honest cost vs value check, using only what Noom’s own pages say. Prices and facts checked 22 November 2026 against Noom’s official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: It depends on what you are buying. $70 a month, $169 for 4 months, or $209 a year. What you get: daily CBT lessons, a coach, food colors, logging, and weigh-ins. Worth it if you want structure and a coach and will use it for 16 weeks; not worth it if you want a set meal plan or the cheapest tracker. For adults 18 and over only.

“Is it worth it?” is the question every Noom review is really asking, and most reviews answer it with someone else’s result — a number of pounds, a before-and-after photo, a story. This guide does not do that. We have never used Noom, we have no user testimonials on this site, and we do not promise results. What we can do honestly is lay out the two sides of the deal: what the money buys, and what it does not buy. The verdict is yours to make from that — and this guide tells you exactly what it costs to find out.

What Noom costs

Three prices carry the decision: $70 a month on the month-to-month plan, $169 up front for the 4-month plan ($42.25 a month), and $209 up front for the annual plan ($17.40 a month). There are twelve plans in total, from one month to twelve months, and every one is billed up front and auto-renews. Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and a plan usually runs for around 16 weeks (checked 22 November 2026).

The 4-month plan is the number to judge the value against: $169 for roughly the length of the typical program. The annual plan is cheaper per month but only if you use the full year; the monthly plan is the most flexible but the most expensive per month. The full maths — including what you save against monthly billing, and the catch that the savings only exist if you stay — is in Noom Monthly vs Annual Plans.

What you actually get

Five things, and they are the same on every plan.

  • Daily CBT lessons. Short lessons, around 5–15 minutes a day, built on cognitive behavioral therapy, covering your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. They teach you why you eat the way you do, and they build new habits in small steps — no willpower, no food restrictions.
  • A coach. A coach trained in the science behind behavior change, who looks at your food log, suggests different foods, and helps you stay motivated when you feel stuck. Coaching is included at no extra cost, and coaches adapt to your needs — checking in once every couple of weeks is fine, or more often if you need it.
  • The color system. Every food you log is green, yellow, or orange by calorie density, with a suggested daily allotment for each. No food is off limits.
  • Logging. Log everything you eat, after each meal and snack or at the end of the day — your choice — and the tracker tells you the color of every food.
  • Weigh-ins. A daily weigh-in is an option, not a requirement; daily weigh-ins help by reducing the anxiety you feel when you step on the scale (checked 22 November 2026).

Supporting parts: exercise starting from 200 steps a day, food first, syncing with Apple Watch, Fitbit, and Garmin; a recipe database of thousands of recipes with information about how “green” they are; and a group of Noomers for inspiration, advice, or to share wins. The lessons, the coach, the colors, the log, and the weigh-in are the five parts; the rest supports them (checked 22 November 2026).

What you don’t get

A set meal plan. The recipe database is a library, not a plan: thousands of recipes with information about how they fit into your caloric range, but no one tells you what to eat on Tuesday. If you want your meals planned for you, this is not that program.

A guaranteed one-to-one coach. Coaching is included, and your coach looks at your log — but whether coaching is one-to-one, how quickly a coach replies, and whether you always talk to the same coach are all to be verified. The pages do not say. Do not pay for the program on the assumption of a dedicated personal coach unless you have verified that first.

Medical supervision. Noom Weight is a behavior-change program built on CBT and coaching, not medical treatment. The coaches are trained in the science behind behavior change, not described as doctors. If you have a medical condition, talk to a clinician before starting a weight-loss program. Weight-loss medication is a separate program — Noom Med — and this guide does not cover it.

A quick fix. The program usually runs for around 16 weeks, and the lessons are about changing how you think about food, not about a shortcut. Anyone selling Noom as fast is selling something Noom itself does not claim.

Who it is worth it for

People who want structure. If you have tried tracking on your own and stopped, the daily lesson plus the coach plus the log is a structure that holds you in the program. The lessons arrive every day, the log waits for you after every meal, and the coach checks in. That is what the money buys: a system, not an app.

People who want a coach. If the part you are missing is a person — someone who looks at your log and suggests foods, someone to talk to when you feel stuck — the coach is the part of Noom that a free tracker cannot give you. Whether the coaching is one-to-one is to be verified, but coaching of some form is included at no extra cost.

People who will use it for 16 weeks. The value only exists if you stay. The 4-month plan roughly matches the program’s usual length of around 16 weeks, and the annual plan only saves money if you use the full year. If you will do the lessons and log every day for four months, $169 is the price of the whole program. If you will not, no plan is worth it.

People who are busy. Noom lists “You’re busy, and don’t have a lot of time to invest in a program” among the people it is right for — and the program is built to match: lessons of 5–15 minutes, logging in a few taps, exercise starting from 200 steps. The program does not ask for hours; it asks for minutes, every day (checked 22 November 2026).

Who it is not worth it for

People who want a set meal plan. If you want someone to tell you what to eat at every meal, Noom does not do that. The recipe database helps you choose; it does not choose for you.

People who want the cheapest tracker. If all you want is to log food and see the numbers, a tracker costs less. MyFitnessPal Premium is $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026) — a cheaper tracker, but it has no coach and no CBT lessons. Paying $169 for Noom to do what a $49.99 tracker does is paying for the coach and the lessons you are not using.

People who will not log. Logging is a key element of the program, and the coach works from your log. If you will not log what you eat, the coach has nothing to look at, the colors have nothing to sort, and the program is lessons without a record. Be honest with yourself about this before you pay.

People who want medication. If you are looking for weight-loss medication, that is Noom Med, a separate program — not Noom Weight, and not covered by this guide.

The cheapest way to find out

Every plan starts with a 7-day initial trial. That is the cheapest way to find out whether the program fits you: seven days of lessons, logging, and coaching, before you commit to a plan. Two things about the trial are to be verified: whether it costs anything, and whether it requires a card. Do not assume the trial is free. Noom calls the trial “no risk”, but the pages do not state whether it costs anything or requires a card, and we do not guess them (checked 22 November 2026).

If the trial convinces you, the 4-month plan at $169 is the sensible first buy: it matches the program’s usual length of around 16 weeks, it is the plan most Noom users have, and it costs $111 less than four months of monthly billing. The annual plan at $209 is cheaper per month, but only buy it if you are sure you will use the full year — the refund policy, if you cancel mid-plan, is to be verified.

Noom vs the alternatives, in one paragraph

WeightWatchers uses a Points system and workshops, with 12,000 recipes and a program built around community and meetings. MyFitnessPal is a tracker: Premium is $49.99 a year (checked 22 November 2026), it logs food and exercise and syncs with your devices, but it has no coach and no CBT lessons. Noom sits between them: more structure and more human support than a tracker, more psychology and less group-meeting than WeightWatchers. Which one is worth it depends on which of those three things you actually want — the tracker, the meetings, or the lessons and the coach. The full comparison is in WeightWatchers vs Noom and Noom vs MyFitnessPal.

Our verdict: Noom is worth it for one kind of person and not for another, and the line between them is whether you will use the coach and the lessons. At $169 for 4 months — roughly the length of the typical program — you are buying daily CBT lessons, a coach who reads your log, and a color system that teaches you the pattern. That is worth it if structure and a person are what you have been missing. It is not worth it if you only want a tracker (MyFitnessPal Premium is $49.99 a year), if you want a set meal plan (Noom does not give you one), or if you will not log — because without the log, the coach has nothing to work from.
