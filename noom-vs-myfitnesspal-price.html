Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price

The two prices side by side, the maths, and what the difference buys. Prices checked 22 November 2026 against the official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: Noom costs $209 a year; MyFitnessPal Premium costs $49.99 a year — about a quarter of the price. Monthly: $70 against $9.99. The $159 difference buys a coach and daily CBT lessons; MyFitnessPal is a tracker with Premium features. Both offer a 7-day trial. For adults 18 and over only.

This is the price page for the comparison in Noom vs MyFitnessPal. For the full program — lessons, coaching, colors, exercise — see The Complete Noom Guide. That guide compares the two programs in full — lessons, coaching, colors, exercise. This page answers one question only: what does each one cost, and what does the difference buy? The answer is simple to state and worth spelling out, because the gap is large: Noom costs about four times as much as MyFitnessPal Premium, and the reason is not the tracker. It is the coach and the lessons. This guide lays out both price lists, works out the difference, and shows exactly what the extra $159 a year buys. We promise no results and no timelines: this guide is about money, not about what either program will do for you.

The maths: $159 a year apart

Annual against annual, the difference is $209 − $49.99 = $159.01 a year. MyFitnessPal Premium costs about a quarter of Noom’s annual price: $49.99 ÷ $209 = 0.239, or about 24%. Monthly against monthly, the difference is $70 − $9.99 = $60.01 a month — and over twelve months of monthly billing, Noom costs $840 against MyFitnessPal’s $119.88 (12 × $9.99), a difference of $720.12. All differences are arithmetic from the checked prices (checked 22 November 2026).

Put the other way: for the price of one year of Noom, you get about four years of MyFitnessPal Premium ($209 ÷ $49.99 = 4.18). For the price of one month of Noom, you get about seven months of MyFitnessPal Premium ($70 ÷ $9.99 = 7.01). The gap is not small and it is not close. The question is not which one is cheaper — that is settled — but whether the extra $159 a year buys something you want.

What the extra $159 buys

The extra $159 a year buys two things MyFitnessPal does not offer: a coach and daily CBT lessons.

A coach. Noom’s coach is trained in the science behind behavior change, looks at your food log, suggests different foods to try, and helps you stay motivated when you feel stuck. Coaching is included at no extra cost, and coaches adapt to your needs — checking in once every couple of weeks is fine, or more often if you need it. Whether coaching is one-to-one, how quickly a coach replies, and whether you always talk to the same coach are all to be verified. MyFitnessPal’s Premium page lists features, not coaching: the page offers no coaching service — its “dietitian and trainer” line is a marketing comparison, not a coach you can talk to.

Daily CBT lessons. Short lessons, around 5–15 minutes a day, built on cognitive behavioral therapy, covering your triggers, emotional and stress eating, what to do instead of reaching for a snack, mindfulness, choosing foods, genuine hunger versus “head hunger”, and plateaus. They teach you why you eat the way you do, and they build new habits in small steps — no willpower, no food restrictions. MyFitnessPal’s Premium page lists no lessons at all.

The color system and the group come with the coach and the lessons: every food you log is green, yellow, or orange by calorie density, with a suggested daily allotment for each, and a group of Noomers for inspiration, advice, or to share wins. MyFitnessPal has its own food database and its own logging — the tracking is comparable — but the teaching and the person are what Noom adds, and they are what the $159 pays for (checked 22 November 2026).

What MyFitnessPal gives you for $49.99

MyFitnessPal Premium costs $49.99 a year, or $9.99 a month, and a free version exists with basic food and exercise tracking. Premium adds barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking, an ad-free experience, meal scan, intermittent fasting, and food insights. That is the whole list as the page states it: a tracker, made faster and more detailed. There is no coaching service, no lessons, and no color system on the page (checked 22 November 2026).

For many people that is enough. If you already know how to eat and only need to record it, $49.99 a year buys you a good recorder. The free version records the basics for nothing; Premium removes the ads, adds the barcode and voice logging, and lets you set your own macros. What it does not do is teach you, check on you, or look at your log and suggest something different. That is the line between a tracker and a program, and it is the line the $159 sits on.

Trials compared

Both programs offer a 7-day trial, and the terms are not the same. MyFitnessPal’s trial is free to start, for people who have never upgraded to Premium or never started a Premium trial before. Its page states the terms plainly: the first Premium subscription charge lands on your credit card at the end of the trial unless you cancel before then, and once the trial ends the subscription auto-renews unless you cancel. You cancel in the Settings menu, in the App Store, or in Google Play, depending on where you signed up (checked 22 November 2026).

Noom’s trial is a 7-day initial trial, and Noom calls it “no risk”. Whether it costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified — the official pages do not state the trial’s fee or card requirement, and we do not guess them. So MyFitnessPal’s trial mechanics are confirmed and Noom’s are not: if you want to try before you pay, MyFitnessPal tells you exactly what will happen on day eight, and Noom does not — yet. Note the trial end date on the day you sign up, for either program: both plans auto-renew.

Who should pay more, who should pay less

Pay more — Noom at $209 a year — if you want a person and a curriculum. If you have tried tracking on your own and stopped, the daily lesson plus the coach plus the log is a structure that holds you in the program. The $159 is the price of that structure, and it is the only thing on this page that MyFitnessPal cannot sell you at any price.

Pay less — MyFitnessPal Premium at $49.99 a year — if you only want to track. If you know what to eat and only need to record it, the tracker does the job for a quarter of the price. The free version does the basics for nothing; Premium is worth it only if you want the barcode and voice logging, the custom macros, and the ad-free experience.

Pay nothing yet if you are not sure. Both programs offer a 7-day trial. MyFitnessPal’s terms are confirmed; Noom’s fee and card requirement are still to be verified. Try the one whose trial terms you can read before you start.

Our verdict: MyFitnessPal Premium is the cheaper program by a wide margin — $49.99 a year against Noom’s $209 — and for pure tracking it is the better buy. Noom is worth the extra $159 only if you will use the two things it adds: the coach and the daily CBT lessons. If you want a person who looks at your log and a lesson every day, pay the $209 (or the $169 4-month plan, which is roughly the length of the typical program). If you only want to record what you eat, pay the $49.99 — or nothing at all, on the free version. The price gap is real. So is the gap in what you get.

Frequently asked questions

How much cheaper is MyFitnessPal than Noom?

Annual: $49.99 against $209 — $159.01 less, about a quarter of the price. Monthly: $9.99 against $70 (checked 22 November 2026).

What does MyFitnessPal Premium cost?

$49.99 a year, or $9.99 a month. The annual plan is listed as 58% savings over the Monthly plan (checked 22 November 2026).

Does MyFitnessPal have a free version?

Yes. A free version exists with basic food and exercise tracking. Premium adds barcode and voice logging, custom macro tracking, an ad-free experience, meal scan, and more (checked 22 November 2026).

Do Noom and MyFitnessPal both have a free trial?

Both offer a 7-day trial. MyFitnessPal’s is free to start for first-time users and auto-renews unless cancelled; Noom’s trial fee and card requirement are to be verified (checked 22 November 2026).

Does MyFitnessPal have a coach?

No. The Premium page lists features, not coaching. Noom’s coach looks at your food log and suggests foods (checked 22 November 2026).

Which is better, Noom or MyFitnessPal?

It depends on what you want: structure and a coach (Noom) or tracking at a lower price (MyFitnessPal). The full comparison is in our Noom vs MyFitnessPal guide (checked 22 November 2026).
