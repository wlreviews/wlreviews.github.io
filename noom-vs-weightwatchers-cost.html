Noom vs WeightWatchers Cost

The two prices side by side, what each one buys, and the new Black Friday offer, ending 4 December 2026. Facts checked 27 November 2026 against Noom’s and WeightWatchers’ official pages listed in Sources at the end of this article. Anything we could not verify on an official page is marked “To be verified”.

The short answer: WeightWatchers Core is cheaper than Noom on price: $5 for the first month, then $10 a month on a 12-month plan, $115 in the first year, against Noom’s $209 a year. Core auto-renews yearly at $10 a month ($120 a year). Noom’s cheapest monthly average is $17.40 a month on its annual plan, and $42.25 a month on the 4-month plan most users take. But the prices buy different things: Noom includes daily CBT lessons, a coach, and the color system; WeightWatchers Core is built on the Points system, with nutrition guidance and recipes and a two-week trial of Workshops. The Core+ plan is $10 for the first month, then $20 a month, with the offer ending 4 December 2026 and auto-renewal at $20 a month. For adults 18 and over only.

Price is the first thing most people compare, and it is the easiest thing to get wrong — because the two programs sell time in different lengths. Noom’s typical plan is about four months; WeightWatchers Core is sold as a twelve-month plan. Compare a month against a month and you get one answer; compare what you actually pay for the time you actually use and you get another. This guide does both, with the arithmetic shown, and says nothing about results — neither program promises any.

How this guide works

Every price was checked against an official page — never from memory, never from a roundup article. Anything we could not verify is labelled “To be verified” rather than guessed. The full method is on the About page. For the full comparison — not just price — see WeightWatchers vs Noom.

The same four months, and the same year

Four months. Noom’s typical plan usually runs for around 16 weeks, and most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription: $169. Four months of WeightWatchers Core is $5 for the first month plus 3 × $10 = $35 (our calculation). The difference is $169 − $35 = $134. For the same four months, Noom costs $134 more.

Twelve months. Noom’s annual plan is $209. Twelve months of WeightWatchers Core is $5 plus 11 × $10 = $115 in the first year (our calculation). The difference is $209 − $115 = $94 a year. For a full year, Noom costs $94 more. Once Core renews at $10 a month, twelve months cost $120 and the difference is $89 a year.

Month against month. Noom’s monthly plan is $70 a month, which is 7 times the $10 a month of WeightWatchers Core ($70 ÷ $10 = 7, our calculation). But that is the least useful comparison of the three: most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and Noom’s own cheapest monthly average is $17.40 on the annual plan — $7.40 a month more than Core ($17.40 − $10 = $7.40, our calculation).

One surprise. The Core+ offer, at $10 for the first month and then $20 a month, costs $10 plus 11 × $20 = $230 in the first year (our calculation) — $21 a year more than Noom’s $209 annual plan. At the offer price, the more expensive WeightWatchers plan costs more than Noom’s cheapest plan. The offer ends 4 December 2026, and Core+ then auto-renews yearly at $20 a month ($240 a year).

What each price buys

Noom. Daily lessons built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), around 5–15 minutes from day 1; a coach who can look at your food log and suggest different foods to try, and who checks in once every couple of weeks, or more often if you are struggling; the color system — green, yellow, orange, roughly 30/45/25, no food off limits; a log for everything you eat; and a daily weigh-in as an option, not a requirement. There is no set meal plan. For the full explanation, see The Complete Noom Guide.

WeightWatchers Core. The Points system — WeightWatchers calls it the #1 doctor-recommended Points program; nutrition guidance and recipes, including a recipe library of over 12,000 recipes (checked 20 November 2026); and a two-week trial of Workshops, virtual and in-person, when you purchase a Core membership. Unlimited Workshops and unlimited Fitness Content are Core+ features, not Core features. Access to Registered Dietitians is a Core+ and Med+ feature, not a Core feature: the plans page comparison table marks it “Included” for Core+ and Med+ and “Not included” for Core (checked 23 November 2026).

The difference in kind matters more than the difference in dollars. Noom sells daily teaching and a coach; WeightWatchers sells a points system and workshops. A person who wants someone to teach them why they eat pays Noom’s price for the lessons; a person who wants a system for tracking what they eat pays Core’s price for Points. Neither is a cheaper version of the other — they are different things at different prices (checked 23 November 2026).

Renewals and trials

Noom. Plans are prepaid and auto-renew. The 7-day initial trial comes first; whether the trial costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is to be verified. Exact cancellation steps and the refund policy if you cancel mid-plan are also to be verified.

WeightWatchers Core. The plans page footnote states the renewal prices outright: Core auto-renews yearly at $10 a month, and Core+ auto-renews yearly at $20 a month (checked 27 November 2026). The previous offer (Core $10 a month, Core+ $20 a month, ending 26 November 2026) has been replaced by the Black Friday offer, and the footnote now governs. Whether the Core plan itself has a free trial is to be verified; the two-week Workshop trial is a trial of Workshops, not of the plan.

Who it suits, and who it does not suit

Noom suits you if you want daily teaching and a coach. The lessons, the coach, and the color system are what the extra $94 a year buys over Core in the first year ($89 a year once Core renews at $10 a month). If you have tried diets before and want a different approach rather than another set of rules, see Noom for People Who Have Tried Diets Before.

WeightWatchers Core suits you if you want the cheapest way into a structured program. At $5 for the first month, then $10 a month, it is the lowest-priced plan on this page by a clear margin, and it includes the Points system and the recipe library. It does not suit you if you want daily lessons or a coach who looks at your food log — Core has neither of those two things.

Neither suits you if you only want to count calories. MyFitnessPal Premium is $49.99 a year, which is less than a third of Noom’s $209 and less than half of Core’s $115 in the first year ($120 on renewal) — and it does that one job. For the price comparison, see Noom vs MyFitnessPal Price (checked 23 November 2026).

What it costs

The cheapest way to try Noom is the 7-day initial trial, then the $169 4-month plan, which is roughly the length of the typical program. The cheapest way to try WeightWatchers is Core at $5 a month for the first month, then $10 a month, on a 12-month plan — $115 in the first year, renewing at $10 a month ($120 a year), with a two-week trial of Workshops included. The Core+ offer at $10 a month for the first month, then $20 a month, ends on 4 December 2026 and auto-renews at $20 a month, so do not budget on those rates for Core+ past that date (checked 27 November 2026).

Our verdict: WeightWatchers Core is cheaper — $115 in the first year against Noom’s $209, a difference of $94 ($120 on renewal, a difference of $89), and $35 against $169 for the same four months. But the prices buy different things, and the cheaper plan is not the better plan for everyone: Noom’s extra money buys daily CBT lessons and a coach who looks at your food log, and Core’s lower price buys the Points system, recipes, and a two-week trial of Workshops. If you want teaching, take Noom’s $169 4-month plan. If you want the cheapest structured program, take Core at $5 for the first month, then $10 a month. Core+ at $10 for the first month, then $20 a month, is still live, but the offer ends 4 December 2026 and it auto-renews at $20 a month — do not budget on those rates for Core+ past that date.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Noom or WeightWatchers?

WeightWatchers Core is cheaper on price alone: $5 for the first month, then $10 a month on a 12-month plan, $115 in the first year, against $209 a year for Noom. Core auto-renews yearly at $10 a month ($120 a year). The cheapest Noom monthly average is $17.40 a month on its annual plan (checked 27 November 2026).

How much is Noom for 4 months?

$169 for 4 months, $42.25 a month. Most Noom Weight users have a 4-month subscription, and a plan usually runs for around 16 weeks (checked 23 November 2026).

How much is WeightWatchers Core+ now?

Core+ is $10 a month for the first month, then $20 a month on a 12-month plan, auto-renewing yearly at $20 a month. The offer ends 4 December 2026, according to the footnote on the WeightWatchers plans page (checked 27 November 2026).

Does WeightWatchers Core renew at $10 a month?

Yes. Core is $5 a month for the first month, then $10 a month, and auto-renews yearly at $10 a month, according to the footnote on the WeightWatchers plans page (checked 27 November 2026).

What does the Noom price include that WeightWatchers Core does not?

Daily CBT lessons and a coach who looks at your food log. WeightWatchers Core is built on the Points system, with nutrition guidance and recipes and a two-week trial of Workshops. Neither program promises results (checked 23 November 2026).

Is there a free trial for either program?

Noom starts with a 7-day initial trial. Whether it costs anything, and whether it requires a card, is still to be verified. WeightWatchers Core includes a two-week trial of Workshops; whether the Core plan itself has a free trial is still to be verified (checked 23 November 2026).
